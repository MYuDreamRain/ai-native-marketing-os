# System 2 — The Executive LinkedIn OS

*A personal LinkedIn presence run as an operating system: one strategy document as source of truth, a fixed cadence, and a feedback loop that rewrites the strategy.*

Executive brands die two deaths: the leader has no time to write, or an assistant writes generic content in nobody's voice. An AI writer solves the first and worsens the second, unless the fix is the operating system around the writer, not the writer.

## The architecture

```
strategy bible ──► weekly topic slate ──► draft (in my voice) ──► audience pre-read ──► my edit/approve ──► publish
      ▲                                                                                                        │
      └───────────────── weekly intel scan + "what worked" review ◄────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**One bible, versioned.** Positioning, audience, four pillars with fixed weightings, cadence, voice constraints, banned moves: one document the agent reads before every draft. Change strategy once; every future draft inherits it. No strategy that lives in chat history survives a month. This one is three months old and on its fourth major revision — the most recent one added a fourth pillar, an analysis lane the market research argued for, and rebalanced the weightings across the whole slate. The feedback loop below doesn't decorate the bible; it rewrites it.

**Fixed cadence, pillar-weighted.** Four posts a week, allocated across pillars by percentage. The weighting is a portfolio decision made once, not a weekly debate. The agent proposes the slate against the quota; I reorder or veto. Analysis pillars carry an evidence standard the draft is checked against before it reaches me: a primary-source figure, one first-hand observation only the author could supply, zero unsourced round numbers.

**Constraints as spec.** Standing rules written in enforceable form: no manifesto voice, no engagement-bait hooks, every post must survive the "would I say this to a colleague's face" test. A rule the agent can't check mechanically isn't a rule yet; it's a future incident (see [the rulebook](../rulebook.md)).

**Audience pre-read.** Before I see a draft, the agent re-reads it as the specific person the pillar targets and reports what that reader would skim, doubt, or screenshot. It catches "technically fine, lands wrong" before it costs an edit round.

Two loops close the system: a **daily comment digest** (inbound engagement compressed to a five-minute decision, with drafted reply options) and a **weekly intel scan** whose conclusions get written back into the bible as amended strategy, not filed as a report.

## The firewall

I also steer the company's LinkedIn. Hard rule in the [governance charter](../guardrails.md): the personal system and the company system share no strategy, no drafts, no learnings, in either direction. It bit us once; now the agent enforces the wall on itself.

## Why this transfers

Any executive presence (a founder on X, a CTO's conference talks) reduces to the same parts: strategy as a versioned document the agent must read, cadence as quota, taste as enforceable constraints, engagement as a compressed daily decision, and performance data that amends the strategy instead of decorating a dashboard.
