# System 7 — The Social Selling Pipeline

*The [social listening engine](06-social-listening-engine.md) watches the whole market. This system runs social selling against a named list: the people who matter, warmed one approved touch at a time.*

A curated list of high-value people (prospects, partners, community figures) whose public activity the agent monitors. When one of them posts something worth engaging, the agent drafts a comment in the account owner's register, attaches the relationship context, and queues it for approval. Nothing posts without an explicit, per-item, written yes.

```
curated list ──► feed monitor ──► engagement-worthy? ──► draft in owner's register ──► review queue ──► human approves by ID ──► post ──► relationship memory updated
```

**A list, not a firehose.** The market-wide listener triages everything; this loop watches only people chosen by name, scored against our customer profile. Small list, high attention. Adding someone to the list is itself a reviewed decision.

**Per-relationship memory.** Every touch is logged: what we commented, what they replied, what they've posted lately. The next draft is written with that history in front of the agent, so touch five sounds like a continuing conversation, not a fifth cold opener.

**The review queue is the product.** Numbered items with full context: the post, the draft, why this person, what we said last time. Approval is by ID ("approve 91, 93"); rejection takes a reason, and rejection reasons feed the drafting rules. Same principle as the [decision board](../decision-board.md): make the human decision as small as possible, never make it for them.

**Sequencing as architecture.** Staged relationship plays (view, engage, connect, message) where each escalation is a separate approval, never an automated cascade. Later stages are deliberately harder to approve, because a wrong DM costs more than a wrong comment.

## The hard rules

This system operates *as a person*, on their accounts. It is the highest-trust thing an agent can do, so it runs under the strictest rules in the [governance charter](../guardrails.md):

1. **Zero autonomous posting.** Not "low-risk items auto-post." Zero. The standing instruction from day one: *do not send anything yet*. Every item since has required its own yes.
2. **The human is the author of record.** A draft they wouldn't have written gets rejected, and the rejection makes the next draft better.
3. **Identity separation is absolute.** Which account, which voice, which audience: fixed per item and checked before queueing. Our one identity incident produced a permanent rule the same morning.

## Honest scope

A systems story, not a results story: relationship equity compounds over quarters, and we don't dress it up with vanity engagement numbers. What I can defend: the cost of being present in fifty important people's feeds dropped from "a job nobody did" to minutes of approvals a day, with a human's explicit yes on every touch.
